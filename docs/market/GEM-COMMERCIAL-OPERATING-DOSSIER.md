**Snapshot date:** 2026-09-13  
**Canonical repository:** `support371/gem-enterprise`

**Implementation branch:** `chatgpt/commercial-operating-system`

## 1. Objective

Build one governed commercial lifecycle for GEM Enterprise without duplicating the systems already present in the canonical platform.

Target lifecycle:

`DISCOVER → ATTRACT → CAPTURE → QUALIFY → PROPOSE → CONVERT → ONBOARD → DELIVER → SUPPORT → SUCCESS → EXPAND → RENEW → REFER → LEARN`

GEM remains authoritative for eligibility, scope, entitlement, approvals, customer workspace access, audit evidence, and service activation.

External research, analytics, email, scheduling, CRM, support, or publishing tools may enrich the workflow only when they solve a verified gap and preserve GEM as the system of record.

## 3. Current founding offer

### GEM Business Security & Operations Review

Current public founding price: **$199**.

The live scope is deliberately bounded:

- one business and one primary domain;
- initial review for teams up to 10 people;
- structured security/operations intake;
- identity and access review;
- public-facing website/exposure review;
- business-operations review;
- incident-readiness review;
- data/compliance readiness;
- AI/automation opportunities;
- prioritized findings;
- 30-day action plan;

The customer may fix findings internally or request a separately scoped GEM engagement afterward. The $199 offer is therefore an **entry product**, not the entire commercial model.

Reference framework for risk vocabulary: NIST SP 1300, *Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: Small Business Quick-Start Guide*. Use it as guidance; do not represent a GEM review as NIST certification.

Useful public references:

- `https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-20-small-business-quick-start-guide`

Use current source wording and dates; never convert aggregate threat statistics into claims that a specific prospect is compromised.

## 5. Preliminary ICP ranking

This ranking is an operating hypothesis built from the current offer boundaries, existing Workbench segmentation, public-company research, sales friction, and likely expansion value. It must be updated with actual conversion data.

### 1. Professional services

Examples: law firms, legal operations, accounting/advisory and specialist recruiting firms.

Why first:

- compact teams fit the founding scope;
- heavy dependence on identity, email, client/candidate data and trusted communications;
- owner/partner decision-makers are usually reachable;
- security and operational continuity are understandable business risks;
- strong expansion path into access control, policy/readiness, monitoring and remediation.

### 2. Property / real-estate operations

Examples: property managers, development/investment firms, PropTech, real-estate marketing, buyer/specialist platforms.

Why second:

- account and payment-diversion concerns are commercially understandable;
- distributed vendors/contractors/shared systems create operational dependencies;
- strong fit with GEM's existing property-risk capability;
- potential expansion from cyber/operations review into property-risk coordination.

### 3. Logistics / field operations

Examples: freight forwarders, drayage/warehousing, carriers, supply-chain staffing.

Why third:

- availability and communication failures affect real operations quickly;
- many third-party handoffs and shared systems;
- bounded review can identify fragile access/process dependencies without intrusive testing;
- practical expansion path into resilience, monitoring and incident readiness.

Do not target all industries equally until conversion data proves they deserve equal effort.

## 8. Post-conversion gap closed by this branch

Before this branch, the canonical pipeline was strong through conversion/onboarding but lacked a dedicated customer-success/renewal/referral layer.

This branch adds:

### Workspace-scoped customer-success profile

Tracks:

- lifecycle state;
- health state;
- outcome state;
- satisfaction score;
- outcome summary;
- last review;
- next review;
- optional primary project;
- optional responsible user.

### Explicit lifecycle actions

Types:

- FOLLOW_UP
- OUTCOME
- EXPANSION
- RENEWAL
- REFERRAL
- WIN_BACK

These are planning/evidence objects only. They do **not** activate service, charge a customer, send marketing, publish a testimonial, or bypass normal qualification/entitlement rules.

### Operator surface

`/app/admin/customer-success`

Allows an authorized admin to initialize/update a converted client's success record and maintain explicit follow-up actions against the canonical workspace/project identity.

## 10. Public-claims launch gate

The controlled-launch copy on the homepage, Services, Company, Hub and Business Review is appropriately cautious. However, the live Resources and Privacy surfaces contain stronger statements that require evidence reconciliation before aggressive distribution.

Examples requiring verification include claims about:

- content being curated by a GEM analyst/research team;
- client-available proprietary automation tools;
- expanded regional incident-response units;
- continuous Security Operations Center monitoring;
- SIEM/IDS/IPS coverage;
- regular third-party penetration tests/security audits;
- AES-256 data-at-rest encryption across the represented platform scope;
- MFA/PAM/access-review assertions where the public wording is broader than verified deployment evidence.

Do not silently rewrite legal/privacy commitments as a marketing task. Route them through the public-claims evidence process and appropriate legal/security review. Until evidence exists, do not amplify these statements in campaigns.

## 11. Communication governance

The commercial system must distinguish:

- transactional/service communication;
- one-to-one professional outreach;
- marketing campaign email;
- social/community publishing;
- SMS/phone where enabled;
- referral/testimonial requests.

No channel should infer consent from a different channel.

Bulk outreach is not part of the founding First-20 workflow. The existing Workbench remains one-to-one and human-personalized.

For U.S. commercial email, maintain truthful sender/subject information and applicable opt-out handling. Use current FTC guidance before scaling campaign delivery.

Reference: `https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business`
